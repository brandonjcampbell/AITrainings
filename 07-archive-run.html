Archive Run

The model knows a lot about the world before the outbreak and nothing about your camp's logbooks. You can't paste the whole archive into every question either. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) finds the few passages that matter and hands them to the model with the question, so it answers from your documents and cites them.

Who it's for

Engineers building question answering, search or assistants over their own documents.

Time

About 75 minutes, half hands-on.

Bring

Python 3.10+, and Ollama with llama3.2 and nomic-embed-text pulled (Field Manual 4).

  • Use RAG for facts that live in documents.

    Policies, manuals, tickets, reports. Especially when they change or are too many to fit in one prompt.

  • Not for everything.

    A few short documents fit in the prompt directly. Numbers in a database want a SQL tool. Tone and format want examples or fine-tuning.

  • Retrieval is most of the work.

    If the right passage never reaches the model, no prompt can fix the answer. Measure retrieval on its own.

Part 1 · The supply chain

The RAG pipeline

Two halves. Indexing runs ahead of time, whenever documents change. Answering runs for every question. Select a stage.

Drill 1 · Cut the rations

See how chunking splits a document

Documents are cut into chunks before embedding, and each chunk is what gets retrieved. Too small and a chunk loses the context that makes it make sense. Too big and one passage carries several topics, so it matches everything weakly. Overlap repeats words at the edges so a sentence isn't lost between two chunks.

Watch the line about tablets and waiting time. At some sizes it splits across two chunks, so neither chunk alone answers "how long do I wait?" Real chunkers also respect headings and paragraphs, which this simple word splitter doesn't.

    Appendix · Pocket card
    
    
    Cheat sheet
    
  
    
  
    INDEX   load → clean → chunk → embed → store (with metadata)
ANSWER  embed question → retrieve top k → (rerank) → prompt with sources → cite

Starting points to tune, not rules:
  chunk size   200–500 words, split on headings and paragraphs
  overlap      10–20% of chunk size
  top k        3–8 chunks
  temperature  0 for factual answers

nomic-embed-text prefixes:
  "search_document: " for chunks, "search_query: " for questions

Upgrades, in the order they usually pay off:
  1. better chunking and cleaning
  2. hybrid search (keyword + vector)
  3. metadata filters (date, source, permissions)
  4. a reranker on the top 20–50 results
  5. query rewriting for vague or multi-part questions

Measure:
  retrieval  hit rate@k: is the right source in the top k?
  answer     correct, and supported by the cited sources?
